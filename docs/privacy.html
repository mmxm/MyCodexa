Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 August 2026 · Applies to the Codexa Android and iOS apps and the Codexa web app

Short version: Codexa is self-hosted software. The app is a client — it only ever talks to the server address you enter when you set it up. The Codexa project does not operate any server, does not collect any of your data, and has no analytics, ads, or trackers built into the app.

Who this policy covers

Codexa is free, open-source software (AGPL-3.0) that you or someone you trust runs on your own server (a home NAS, a small VPS, etc.). The app you installed is a client for that server — it has no independent connection to the Codexa project, and the people who develop Codexa never receive, see, or store any of your data.

What data the app handles, and where it goes

  • Server address, username, and password/session token — entered by you, stored locally on your device (in the app's local storage), and sent only to the server address you configured, to log you in and keep you logged in.
  • Books, covers, reading progress, bookmarks, highlights, and notes — stored on your own server; downloaded books and their progress are also cached locally on your device for offline reading.
  • Reading statistics (time read, pages turned, sessions) — calculated on-device and stored on your own server, for your own account only.

None of the above is sent anywhere except the server address you configured. There is no Codexa-operated backend of any kind.

Optional integrations you control

Codexa can optionally connect to other services, but only if you (or your server's admin) explicitly configure them — none are contacted by default:

  • OPDS catalogues you add (e.g. Calibre-Web, Komga, Kavita) — the app talks to whichever catalogue URL you enter.
  • A BookOrbit server you connect, for library browsing and optional two-way sync of highlights, sessions, and progress.
  • KOReader sync — either your Codexa server's own built-in sync, or an external KOSync-compatible server you point it at.
  • Single sign-on (Google, Apple, or a self-hosted provider) — only if your server's admin enables it; authentication happens directly between your device and that provider.

In every case, these connections go directly from your device (or your own server) to the service you specified — the Codexa project is not an intermediary and does not see any of that traffic.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs are built into the app.
  • No advertising, no ad identifiers, no ad networks.
  • No data is sold or shared with third parties by the Codexa project, because the Codexa project never receives any data to begin with.

Permissions

The Android app requests only INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — needed to talk to the server you configure and to detect when connectivity is restored. It does not request access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone, or files outside what you explicitly pick with the system file/document picker (e.g. when uploading a font or book on an admin account).

Children's privacy

Codexa does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children, because it does not collect personal information at all — everything described above is stored on the server you or your household controls, not on any Codexa-operated system.

Your server's own responsibilities

Because you (or your admin) run the actual server, you are the one in control of that data — backups, retention, and access to it are up to how you operate your own server. This policy covers the app's own behaviour; it isn't a substitute for securing your server if you expose it to the internet.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted at this same URL with a new "last updated" date above.

Contact

Questions about this policy or how Codexa handles data can be raised as an issue on the project's repository: github.com/thehijacker/codexa/issues.
